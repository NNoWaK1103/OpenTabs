OpenTabs — install the preview on your own iPhone

This package contains the SwiftUI app source, a generated Xcode project, and
its prebuilt ARM64 Kotlin framework. You do not need Java, Gradle, XcodeGen,
Homebrew, or a paid Apple Developer membership to use this testing package.
An internet connection is needed for Apple's free development signing setup.

1. Extract OpenTabs-iPhone-Test.zip and open the extracted folder.
2. Double-click OpenTabs.xcodeproj. If Xcode asks whether you trust this
   downloaded project, confirm only if you obtained it from OpenTabs CI.
3. In Xcode > Settings > Accounts (or Apple Accounts), add your Apple account.
4. Click the blue OpenTabs project in the left sidebar, then choose the
   OpenTabs app under TARGETS. Open Signing & Capabilities.
5. Leave Automatically manage signing checked and choose your Personal Team.
   Change Bundle Identifier to something unique, for example:
   com.timcarey.opentabs.preview
   If Apple says that identifier is unavailable, choose another unique value.
6. Connect your iPhone by cable, unlock it, and accept Trust This Computer.
7. Select the OpenTabs scheme and your actual iPhone in Xcode's top toolbar.
   This package contains a device framework only; do not choose a simulator.
8. If asked, enable Settings > Privacy & Security > Developer Mode on your
   iPhone, restart, and confirm. The setting may appear after pairing in Xcode.
9. Press the triangular Run button in Xcode (or Command-R).
10. If iOS asks you to trust your developer identity, follow its prompt under
    Settings > General > VPN & Device Management. Then run again from Xcode.

Once installed, you can disconnect your phone and open the app normally.
With a free Personal Team, signing expires after seven days; repeat Run in
Xcode to refresh it. Updating the app also requires another Run from Xcode.

Try opening First steps, scrolling in both directions, and the Larger/Smaller
buttons. This preview has three original exercises. Saved libraries, file
import, online search, and website browsing are not yet implemented on iOS.

This is a device-testing project, not an already-signed installer or App Store
release. CI validates it with Xcode 26.0. A newer Xcode still needs to build
and sign the Swift app; the included Kotlin framework does not rebuild locally.
Newer-Xcode physical-device testing is not established by the CI check.
